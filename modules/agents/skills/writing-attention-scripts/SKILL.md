# writing attention scripts

give the listener a reason to start, something new in each passage, and an ending that delivers what the opening promised.

## before drafting

identify the listener, target length and one thing they should understand by the end. infer these from the request when possible. use the supplied source; do not reopen its investigation.

read [the writing playbook](references/playbook.md), then draft the spoken script. it explains how to choose an opening, order the explanation, mark emphasis and cut sentences that do no work.

## check the draft

- read it aloud. replace phrases you stumble over; split clauses that need a second breath.
- compare claims with the source. keep any condition or uncertainty that changes their meaning. a stronger opening must not turn a possible result into a demonstrated one.
- read the opening and emphasized phrases on their own. they must not mislead someone who misses the surrounding explanation.
- check length at a plausible speaking pace. label duration as an estimate; if necessary detail will not fit, surface the length/scope tradeoff.

## return the words first

use plain text unless the caller supplies a format. separate the narration from notes so a reader or speech engine cannot mistake directions for spoken words.

after the script, include only useful notes: selected phrases to emphasize, difficult pronunciations, source locations for consequential claims, and word count/estimated duration. optional caption groupings are editorial suggestions, not separate speech takes or invented word timings.

stop here for script-only requests. do not download footage, install media tools, synthesize speech or render a video. when the user also requests a video, hand the finished script to `making-brainrot-videos`; writing does not otherwise require that skill.

see [selection and behavior cases](references/cases.md) when evaluating the skill. written cases are not evidence that model routing or audience retention improved.
