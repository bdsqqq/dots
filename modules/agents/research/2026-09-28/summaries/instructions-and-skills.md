# instructions, skills, and human-readable outputs

the shared lesson is to tune communication around observed failures, not collect somebody else's setup. these three videos connect instruction scope, skill selection, and output format to a desired outcome: a human can understand, steer, and review the work with fewer repeated corrections.

## candidate recommendations, not setup changes

all seven are **HUNCH** proposals derived from the cited passages:

1. **scope instructions by consumer.** keep personal interaction defaults global; put vocabulary, hazards, and supported surfaces in project guidance. risk: duplicated or stale rules. evaluate matched tasks for missed surfaces and contradictory instructions.
2. **test skill triggers separately from bodies.** use real request phrases plus explicit exclusions; separate filing from monitoring. risk: accidental activation. evaluate positive and near-miss prompts, counting missed and unwanted loads.
3. **derive additions from repeated corrections.** trial one small change against representative failures. risk: overfitting one model or task. evaluate held-out tasks and remove instructions that add cost without reducing corrections.
4. **teach outcomes with paired examples.** lead reports with user problem, result, and evidence. risk: concise prose can conceal uncertainty or invent metrics. evaluate reader comprehension and unsupported-claim counts.
5. **make interviews opt-in and bounded.** inspect repository facts before asking product questions; record unresolved tradeoffs. risk: questionnaire fatigue and leading options. evaluate useful decisions reached per question, not interview length.
6. **choose html for comparison or interaction.** retain markdown for simple durable text; include labels and exportable selections where useful. risk: accessibility failures, misleading visuals, and disclosure through hosting. evaluate comprehension, mobile/keyboard use, factual fidelity, and total time; publication needs separate approval.
7. **borrow individual skills with dependencies understood.** preserve attribution and check harness assumptions before a sandbox trial. risk: imported authority, scripts, or unwanted workflow. evaluate behavior against the same task without the skill; installation count is not success.
